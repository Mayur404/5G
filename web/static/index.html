UNDERSTAND THE EXPERIMENT

Capacity before the crossing.

01 · FORECAST

Look a few intervals ahead.

A synthetic predictor projects motion and estimates a target, arrival interval, and RB demand. Its score is heuristic and can be wrong.

02 · RESERVE

Hold capacity when it is useful.

The deep controller forecasts future position and PRB demand with a trained two-hidden-layer network, then applies a fixed reservation rule. QoS target selection considers signal, capacity and latency. The legacy linear PPO is available for comparison. Pending requests use no capacity; activation can be rejected.

03 · COMPARE

Check what actually happened.

Both policies see identical movement and traffic. Admission needs the slice minimum and a successful radio draw. QoS also needs full demand and latency within its limit.

Slice priorities and modeling assumptions

Each cell schedules uRLLC first, eMBB second, then mMTC, with device ID breaking ties. This is a priority scheduler, not guaranteed slice isolation.

Icons are visual categories; a car, drone, or robot can use any slice. Movement is in abstract world units per interval. Cell regions show nearest-site geography. In QoS mode, eligible neighboring towers can serve a device based on load and signal. A failed handover updates the assigned target but allocates zero PRBs; the next interval reschedules it. This is a capacity model, not a persistent radio connection or packet-level simulation.

There is no simulated RSRP, SINR, A3 trigger, optical backhaul, beamforming, packet loss, or zero-delay transfer. Optional site connections are illustrative.

Math and accounting, explained

Cell capacity

allocated + active holds + free = capacity

Integer RB counts share one scheduling interval. Pending requests are excluded from active holds.

Reservation use

used = admitted × min(demand, matching active hold)

Wrong-target and pending holds cannot serve an arrival. A failed admission consumes zero reserved RBs. Remaining held RBs are released or expire.

Admission and QoS

available = min(demand, matching hold + free)

Admission requires available ≥ slice minimum and a successful radio draw. QoS additionally requires available ≥ demand and modeled latency ≤ slice limit.

Training reward

reward = 2h + 3q − 1.5w − 0.25c

h and q are interval handover admission and QoS fractions (zero with no crossing); w is settled unused RBs / network capacity; c is active held RBs / network capacity. Live playback uses the frozen policy and does not retrain it.

SEPARATE HELD-OUT EVALUATION

A demonstration, with measurable limits.

These small scenarios explain individual decisions. The separate benchmark uses 32 devices and 20 matched seeds. PPO improves on no reservation in that evaluation, while other heuristics achieve higher admission rates at higher reservation cost. This synthetic system-level model is not field validation.
